100 generated city levels, a dragon with no skeleton, and music synthesized from numpy (Godot 4 devlog)

I'm making Bao Boom, a 3D arcade game in Godot 4.4: you're a small dragon in a lantern-lit Chinese town the night before a festival. The guards stole the celebration, and you light every firework on the map before they catch you. 100 levels, 10 districts, one-touch controls, a tail that keeps growing.

The premise is simple. These are the technical problems that weren't.

1. The firework you can't see — The camera looks from the south and above, which reads great until a tall building stands between the camera and the thing you need to find. A firework could sit three cells away and be completely invisible. Fix: an 'open cell' rule in the item placer. A firework counts as visible only if the two cells south of it (y+1, y+2) are free and there's room on the sides. Otherwise the position is rejected. Level design turned out to depend on the view, not just the walkability graph.

2. 100 levels, none placed by hand — Levels are plain JSON grids (2 m per cell: # building, . street, F firework, P start…). A Python generator builds districts, buildings, squares, a guard pen with gates. Then a validator runs on all 100 and fails the build on: disconnected streets, dead ends, unreachable items, huge empty plazas or too-narrow passages. First pass had 56% building density and felt claustrophobic. A thinning script removes buildings from merged blocks and carves passages, re-validating after every step. Result: 47% density, same guarantees.

3. A dragon with no skeleton — The body is rebuilt every frame along the head's recorded path, with procedural meshes, and legs use simple IK. It bends instead of looking like a chain of beads. Controls were the real pain: on a grid, players don't aim at cells. Corner assist nudges the dragon into the opening when it clips a corner (up to ~0.95 m), and wall collision radius cut from 0.8 to 0.55 m while keeping the guard-catch radius at 0.8. Forgiving, but still fair.

4. Guards that don't twitch — Grid AI changes heading instantly when its target changes, and it looks broken. Capping the turn rate and adding smooth braking near gates fixed it — guards became readable, you can predict where they'll turn.

5. Music and textures from scripts — There are no audio files from outside the project. A script synthesizes pentatonic tracks (plucked guzheng, bowed erhu with glissando/vibrato, bass pluck, taiko, wood block) with numpy/scipy — one slow menu track plus five rhythmic level tracks. World textures are scripted too, so the whole look can be regenerated with one command.

Three scars — Level map ran at ~7 FPS on a laptop, caused by an earlier 'optimization'. Fix: bake the background (sky, silhouettes, gates, trees) into one texture. Web build without threads (so it runs where cross-origin isolation isn't available): baked HUD frames/icons into textures, redraw the minimap less often, put distant objects to sleep, skip decor on weak devices. The demo is the same code as the full game, behind a build flag (10 of 100 levels, 2 of 8 skins). Demo contents are frozen and verified by a script, so nothing changes by accident.
